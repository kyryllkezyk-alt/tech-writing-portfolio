# Reversing a failed quality audit

**Role:** Hebrew localization lead · **Area:** Language quality assurance · **Deliverable:** Evidence
documentation and reviewer guidelines

## Situation

A client's external reviewer failed a large Hebrew delivery in a language quality audit. The
failing score put the account at risk and triggered rework at our cost.

## Problem

Most of the flagged "errors" were preferences or misreadings of the client's own style guide:
correct Hebrew gender agreement marked as wrong, approved glossary terms marked as inconsistent,
and right-to-left punctuation that rendered correctly in the product but not in the reviewer's
spreadsheet.

Arguing case by case wasn't working. The process had no shared document defining what counts as an
error.

## What I did

1. **Classified every flagged item** against the client's style guide and glossary, citing the
   exact rule for each one.
2. **Wrote an arbitration document** with a consistent structure per item: segment, reviewer's
   claim, governing rule, evidence, proposed category.
3. **Wrote Hebrew-specific reviewer guidelines** covering gender-neutral phrasing, RTL punctuation,
   and how to check rendering in context rather than in a spreadsheet.
4. **Proposed an error typology** with severity definitions so future disputes had a reference.

## Outcome

- The audit was re-scored and passed.
- The client adopted the reviewer guidelines for later audits.
- Disputed items in later audits dropped sharply, because both sides worked from the same document.

## What this shows

Documentation turned an argument into a process. The fix wasn't better translation; it was a
shared, written definition of quality.
